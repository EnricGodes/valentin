*Iconic profile of the Porsche 911 G series*

Each generation of the Porsche 911 has its own particularities, but if we talk about the G Series, **manufactured from 1973 to 1989**, we find the model that laid **the foundations of the design** that defines each of the models of the brand Stuttgart.

The Porsche 911 of the G Series may be 'the Porsche of Porsches'. Manufactured for no less than 16 years, from 1973 to 1989, from 1975 it was completely galvanized, making it a car for eternity, thanks to its good protection against rust. Possibly, the G Series versions of the 911 are among the most clearly defined sports cars of all time and with a design that continues to be extrapolated to current models.

## Models before Porsche 911 G

Taking a look at its history, 1973 was the last year for the 911 of the F Series. The German firm offered this generation in Coupé and Targa versions. The E versions developed **165 CV**, while the S reached **190 CV.** This power was supplied by a six-cylinder flat-six 2.4 liters. The spearhead of the range and the brand, and also the fastest German-made car in its time, was the **Carrera RS 2.7**. As its name suggests, it was distinguished by its 2.7 liter engine and the legendary "ducktail" rear spoiler, which guaranteed the necessary aerodynamic load on the rear axle. The Carrera RS was developed for homologation in the GT category.

However, time was already taking its toll on him. 10 years after the world premiere of the 911, the 911 E, the 911 S and the Carrera RS 2.7 were still a continuation, both inside and out. Thus, the G Series arrived to take the next step in the evolutionary history of the 911.

![The Porsche 911 G series models](/img/magazine/d7f469a7-img_7-1024x576.jpg)

## Features of the Porsche 911 G series

The new generation of the 911 came onto the market in September 1973. Its new design attracted attention from the first moment: **the bumpers were much larger than before, more rectangular and had distinctive bellows on the sides.** They could withstand small parking bumps without suffering damage up to a speed of 8 km/h. At the front, the indicators were integrated into the bumper for the first time on a 911. This radically changed the design compared to the predecessor, making it look much more modern.

While visual signs of the 60 years could still be seen on the F Series, the new 911 clearly presented itself as a car from the 70. The rear end was hardly changed, but the new bellows bumper, together with a red reflector with the Porsche logo located between the taillights, **created a completely new and significantly more modern appearance.** The new model also benefited from an exhaust system with increased rust protection.

![](/img/magazine/6f4845fd-img_10-1024x576.jpg)

Inside, the style received more defined contours. The **sports seats with integrated headrests,** which are still so familiar today, were first used in 1973. The **covers for door compartments,** which would become a recognized feature of Porsche. The steering wheel and light stalks were new and there were two additional air vents.

On a mechanical level, the Porsche 911 Series G mounted the **six cylinder block Carrera RS 2.7 previous.** He developed a power of **150 CV,** while the 911 S produced **175 CV.** Both versions were equipped with a new K-Jetronic injection from Bosch. The flagship model was still the 911 Carrera, with **210 CV.** It incorporated the engine of the Carrera RS 2.7 and had a maximum speed of 240 km/h. Furthermore, the Carrera It had a rear part 42 mm wider compared to less powerful models, to accommodate the larger rear track. The G series had a gearbox Four-speed gearbox as standard, with a five-speed gearbox available as an option. The basic model and the 911 S They could also be ordered with the gearbox Four-speed Sportomatic, but it was sold in very small quantities.

## Six versions of the Porsche 911 G series

![](/img/magazine/046425b4-img_6-1024x576.jpg)

The generation change also resulted in the creation of new versions, in addition to the traditional RS and Targa, which provided greater versatility to the German sports car.

- **Carrera RS: ** was the first 911 to receive the new 3.0 liter engine. Only 110 units of this powerful 911 of 230 CV were built and was capable of reaching 245 km/h. It was also lighter (weighed 1.060 kg).
- **Turbo:** in 1974 Porsche developed the most powerful production car in its history: the 930 Turbo. Porsche took the base engine from the Carrera RS 3.0 and fitted it with a turbocharger. His difficulty in carrying him around the site was what made him a legend. Initially with 260 CV, in 1977 it was updated to the 3.3 liter engine and the power rose to 300 CV.
- **Targa:** could not be missing. The hardtop was a novelty, offered only in 1974 before being replaced again by the much more practical folding roof. Although the Targa was actually developed to meet US safety regulations, it gained immediate popularity among 911 drivers around the world and soon became a fixture in the Porsche range.
- **Cabriolet:** with a maximum speed of 235 km/h, the same as the Coupé and the Targa, it was one of the fastest convertibles in the world. It accelerated to 100 km/h in just 6,8 seconds. The Cabriolet was also the first 911 in Europe to be equipped as standard with an exterior mirror on the passenger side.
- **Speedster:** Porsche presented the atmospheric 911 Speedster at the Frankfurt Motor Show. In 1988 it was available in two variants, with the normal rear part or the widened one of the Turbo. Only 171 examples of the narrow Speedster were built.

In July 1989, a **Porsche 911 Coupé** marked the end of the G Series. In Grand Prix white, with body of normal width and sliding roof, many described it as "perfect."
