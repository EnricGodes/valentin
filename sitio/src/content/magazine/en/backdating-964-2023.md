## Where it started

After long conversations in the workshop and in the bar, we arrived at an outline both sides agreed on.

A backdating is not something you do by the book. It is not copying, it is not "I want a Singer". There are thousands of details and thousands of scenarios to weigh up, among them what the car will be worth once the project is finished.

That is where Valentin Motors puts the emphasis: that this 911 should have a particular value when it is done, that it should be distinctive and its own thing.

![Work in progress](/img/magazine/19ddc780-21ee72b0-0cc1-4281-b96e-876a5b3d0dc0.jpg)

*The final result, in progress*

### Jordi's view

If I am honest, nobody knows what will happen with these conversions. We do know what a new Singer costs, we know what they fetch used, and we know that homologating a Singer in Spain is impossible, short of a miracle.

We know the work of Gunther Werks, Kienle, Kreizel and the rest.

A backdating is an exercise in style and personality. Whoever commissions one is usually someone you will not sway much: they know what they want, and that is that.

Even so, we draw the red lines ourselves, in favour of whatever may come later.
